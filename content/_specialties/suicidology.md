I am experienced in supporting people through both acute suicidal crisis, as well as chronic, longer-term suicidality. I have been on both sides of the “clipboard” so to speak, both as a giver and as a receiver of professional help during a mental health crisis.

<!--more-->

I remember what interactions with doctors and therapists were helpful, and what things made me feel worse. I hold those experiences close to my heart when I meet others in that same space, and my therapeutic style is informed not only by my direct lived experiences with aspects of the mainstream mental health system that I found to be harmful while I was navigating it. As a result, I have cultivated a very nuanced and revolutionary framework for understanding the real psychological meaning and significance behind suicidal ideation, and for redefining what the language of self-harm is really trying to communicate, and for how to have a more helpful and healing dialogue with those parts of the self.
